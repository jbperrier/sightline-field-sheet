What this means for you

No firm dominates commercial electrical work in the metro. The busiest ten hold a quarter of permits, so a firm with your credentials can win share by getting in front of more GCs.

Your public credentials help you with GCs: Class A license, WBE/SBE certification through CERT, ISNetworld and Browz approval, and OSHA 10/30 trained crews.

S2

Six Activities and What to Expect

Start with activities 1 to 3. They cost mostly time and connect directly to bids. Activities 4 to 6 add reach later.

ActivityWhat you doHow oftenBenchmarkWhat to expectCost
1. Get on more GC bid lists Send a short email to GCs that build your kind of work. Reference a project they built. Four touches: day 1, 4, 10 and 21. Ask a peer or the Minnesota Electrical Association for introductions too. 10 new GCs a week 3.4% average reply. 1 to 2% of emails book a meeting. 5 to 10% reply with strong personalization. Industry benchmark 100 GCs (400 emails) gives about 4 to 8 meetings. Your time
2. Turn meetings into bid invitations After each meeting, send your qualifications, three references, WBE/SBE certificate, safety approvals and insurance. Ask to join the bid list. Many public and institutional projects set WBE/SBE goals, so ask each GC. Within 24 hours of a meeting No published benchmark for invitations per GC. Estimate The calculator assumes 2 to 5 invitations a year from each GC that adds you. Your time
3. Bid the right jobs and track every bid Log each bid: won or lost, price gap, GC, job type. Drop job types you lose by wide margins. Consider a bid platform to see more invitations. Weekly review 30 to 40% win rate on electrical bids. Above 40% often means prices are too low. Industry benchmark At 35%, about 3 bids per win. PlanHub Premier: $1,999 a year (50 miles) to $3,299 (200 miles)
4. Watch planning agendas The Hastings Planning Commission meets the 2nd and 4th Monday. The Development Review Committee meets Tuesdays. Note each commercial or industrial project and call the applicant's GC before design finishes. Repeat for Newport and nearby cities. 30 minutes a week No published benchmark. We estimate 6 to 18 months of lead over the bid. Estimate 1 to 2 early GC conversations a month. Test for 90 days before counting on it. Your time
5. Turn finished jobs into service agreements You already offer maintenance and 24-hour on-call. Offer a yearly inspection and service plan to each building owner when a job closes. Every completed job Service work gross margin: 48 to 55% at top-quartile firms. Commercial bid work: 25 to 32%. Industry benchmark Each service dollar earns about 1.5 to 2 times the margin of a bid dollar. Your time
6. Show up in local search Complete your Google Business Profile, ask every customer for a review, and list your service area. Do the free steps first. Pay for ads only after you see results. Review request after each job Local search marketing runs $1,500 to $4,500 a month for firms with $0.5M to $3M revenue. Leads cost $40 to $300 each. Industry benchmark Feeds service and maintenance calls. It does not feed GC bids. $0 for profile and reviews. $1,500+ a month for paid help.

S5

The First 90 Days

Nothing here has started. Each block is a proposal for you to review.

Days 1 to 30Get ready
  • Build a one-page qualifications sheet with credentials, three references and three project photos.
  • Build a list of 100 GCs that build commercial, industrial and school projects in your area.
  • Start the bid log. Record every bid from the last 12 months if you can.
  • Decide on a bid platform. Check what you already pay for first.
Days 31 to 60Start outreach
  • Contact 10 GCs a week with the four-touch sequence.
  • Send your qualifications packet within 24 hours of every meeting.
  • Start the weekly planning-agenda check.
Days 61 to 90Measure and adjust
  • Compare your results with the scorecard in S6.
  • Replace the calculator's estimates with your own reply rate, meeting rate, invitations and win rate.
  • Keep what works. Drop what does not. Decide whether to add paid help.
