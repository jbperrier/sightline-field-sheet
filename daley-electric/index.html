Who the 1,051 Are: Revenue, Marketing Spend, Agent Wallet

No public file gives revenue per firm, so the tiers below are an estimate. The total is cross-checked against national industry revenue. Pick a spending scenario to see the wallet move.

TierFirmsAvg revenueMarketing / firmAgent wallet / firmAgent wallet, tierFit

How these numbers are built

  • Firm counts: the 1,051 is verified. The split across tiers is my assumption (40% / 42% / 15% / 3%).
  • Revenue per firm: national electrician revenue of about $312B across about 265,000 businesses is $1.1M to $1.2M a firm, but that count includes one-person shops. Class A firms with employees run higher.
  • Cross-check: the tier total of about $3.2B compares with about $3.4B from spreading national revenue by population for a 3.7M-person metro. That population figure is my assumption.
  • Marketing share: agency guides say 4 to 7% of revenue for established firms and 8 to 12% for growth. Those guides sell marketing, so they run high. Commercial subs live on relationships and bids, so the scenarios use 1.5%, 2.5% and 4%.
  • Agent wallet: the slice of marketing money a firm moves to an agent-run outbound and bid-intelligence service. 15%, 25% or 35%. A tier below $6,000 a year is not worth serving.

Limits on this ramp

The base and high cases need warm channels: Daley's referrals, the Minnesota Electrical Association, and GC introductions. Cold email reaches 4 to 8 customers at most.

Delivery cost is not modeled. A service that a person reviews has lower gross margin than software, and hours per customer are unknown. Measure them with the first three customers before setting the price.

Customer counts and prices are planning numbers. Five qualified customers describing the same problem and two paid pilots are still required before this becomes a business.

Phased Implementation Roadmap

Nothing here is built or bought. Each phase is a proposal.

Month 1Foundation
  • Interview Daley: how jobs arrive today, which bid platforms it pays for, which GCs send work, hours spent per bid.
  • One CRM with a sequencing agent. A short page for estimators.
Months 1 to 2Data and lists
  • Enrich the 1,051 DLI contractors with email and size. Rank by fit.
  • Build a GC list from building-permit contractor names.
  • Back-test planning agendas for Hastings and nearby cities: how many listed projects reached bid, and how early.
Month 2 onOutbound
  • Email and LinkedIn to owners and estimators. Human-approved drafts.
  • Ask Daley for introductions to 2 or 3 GCs and 2 or 3 peer subs.
  • Minnesota Electrical Association events. Daley is a member.
OngoingReferrals and feedback
  • Track bids submitted and jobs won by signal source.
  • Review monthly by cost per meeting and per won job, not per lead.
MaturityStanding agent
  • Weekly agenda pulls, project and GC matching, drafted outreach.
  • Watch PlanHub and ConstructConnect for moves into earlier-stage data.
