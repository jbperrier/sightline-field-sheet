Who the ~250 Are: Revenue, Marketing Spend, Agent Wallet

Tier sizes follow the AIA size shares. Revenue per tier is an estimate. Pick a spending scenario to see the wallet move.

How these numbers are built

  • Firm count: about 19,000 US firms times the metro's roughly 1.1% population share, raised by about 20% because the Twin Cities has many design firms. Both steps are my assumptions.
  • Tier split: AIA reports 28% sole, 32% with 2 to 4, 15% with 5 to 9. I split the remaining 25% into 22% (10 to 49) and 3% (50 or more).
  • Revenue per tier: $0.15M, $0.5M, $1.2M, $6M and $60M. My estimates. The total of about $0.85B compares with about $1.15B from spreading US billings by population.
  • Marketing share: PSMJ reports 3.1 to 5.3% of net service revenue. The AIA 2024 Firm Survey reports an average of 6%. The scenarios use 3%, 5% and 7%. Residential-focused firms run higher than institutional ones.
  • Agent wallet: 15%, 25% or 35% of marketing spend moved to an agent-run service. A tier below $6,000 a year is not worth serving. Firms with 50 or more people run business development in-house.

Limits on this ramp

The pool is small. Every customer is 0.8% of it, so reaching 10 customers takes warm introductions: David, AIA Minnesota chapter events, and referrals from builders and realtors.

Delivery cost is not modeled. Personal, property-specific outreach drove David's 10% direct-mail response. A generic version would fall to 1.4 to 2.1%. The service must keep the personal quality, which costs review time.

Architect firms adopt AI slowly: 72% of small firms are reluctant to use it in daily work (AIA 2024). A managed service suits them better than software, which is the point of this model.

Phased Implementation Roadmap

Nothing here is built or bought. Each phase is a proposal for selling to design firms.

Month 1Foundation
  • Ask the Board of Architecture for firm registrations, or build the list from AIA Minnesota and Houzz plus manual checks.
  • Interview 5 design firms beyond David: how leads arrive, what they pay for, what they would pay.
Months 1 to 2Data
  • Run the county feeds weekly: vacant lots, teardown candidates, planning filings.
  • Decide on Northstar MLS access for listed lots and listing agents.
Month 2 onSales to firms
  • Warm introductions first: David, AIA Minnesota events, builder and realtor referrals.
  • Cold email to the 100 to 150 target firms. Expect 1 to 3 customers from it.
OngoingFeedback loop
  • Track meetings booked and jobs won per customer by signal source.
  • Measure review hours per customer to set the price.
