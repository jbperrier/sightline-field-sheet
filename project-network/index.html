Project Network: Who Builds a Twin Cities Project

Every new project brings a design firm, a general contractor and several trades. This sheet counts the firms in each role, sizes what each could spend on an agent-run sales service, and compares ways to enter.

Agent Wallet by Role

Same method as the electrical and design-firm sheets. The plumbing and HVAC revenue tiers copy the electrical tiers, which is an assumption. Pick a scenario.

RoleFirmsTarget firmsWallet, all tiersReachable walletReachable / target firm

How to read this

  • Target firms are small and mid tiers. Micro firms spend too little, and large firms run business development in-house.
  • Reachable wallet is the yearly spend those target firms would move to an agent-run service.
  • Trade rows add up to more than the distinct total, because a firm can hold two licenses. The distinct row removes that overlap by a flat 18%.
  • Plumbing and HVAC firms lean toward residential service work, which needs local marketing more than bid work. Bid and new-construction spend may be lower than these figures.

What each role would do with this record

  • Design and land-planning firms: the plat and engineering come next. No developer is named yet, so the owner is the first contact.
  • Builders: lots are sold after the preliminary plat. The repeat builders in Hastings and Dakota County are the ones to call first.
  • Electrical, plumbing and HVAC firms: their work is probably more than a year away. That is early enough to meet the builder before any bid.
S6

Recommendation and Open Items

ShareOne record, one engine
  • One project record with fields for owner, design firm, GC, trades and stage.
  • One outreach engine: list building, enrichment, drafting and human approval.
SeparateOne offer per role
  • Design firms: projects with no architect yet.
  • Trades: projects with the GC named and a bid coming. One offer with copy for each trade.
  • Builders and GCs: projects and the trades who want them. Not tested.
ChooseThe first buyer
  • If planning filings lead bids by three months or more, start with the trades. The pool is 1,503 firms.
  • If they do not, start with design firms. The signal is earliest for them.
  • The trades win on pool size in either case, so the three-trade motion is the stronger default.
OpenNot yet known
  • Back-test of planning agendas: not complete. The Hastings meeting calendar refused automated access (HTTP 403). Agendas need to be read from city PDFs or by hand.
  • Size of the active GC pool: the DLI registration file lists 9,954 metro contractors and has no work-type or size field. Only 29% of Saint Paul's 262 active new-build and addition contractors match a registration, and 50% match a residential license. Permit activity is the better filter.
  • Revenue per firm for plumbing and HVAC: copied from electrical.
  • Gate before promising anything: five qualified customers per role and two paid pilots.
